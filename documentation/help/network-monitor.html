Help index/Network Monitor

Network Monitor

What this is for

A live view of your connections: Wi-Fi, mobile network, Bluetooth and satellites, with signal graphs and the details of the active link. It also holds checks you can run, such as speed, ping and a scan of your local network, and keeps a history of what you measured.

In this order

  1. Start on the summary. The card at the top 1 says what you are connected to and whether the internet works; below it are your local address 2, your external address 3 and the speed right now 4.
  2. Each tile 5 opens one section. The line on a tile says in words whether the section works on this device, needs a permission, or has nothing to show, so you know before you open it.
  3. Wi-Fi 13, mobile network 18 and Bluetooth 20 show what you are connected to, with a signal graph. Satellites 23 shows how many satellites the phone sees and where it thinks it is.
  4. Internet 27 draws the path from the phone to the internet and the traffic now. Pick what to check 29, then check a resource, scan the local network, look up the external address or measure the speed. A notice 50 may come first.
  5. Tools 35 pings a host, traces its route and scans a range of addresses. Resource speed 42 measures the speed to one of your resources.
  6. Every check you run is kept in History 45. Export it 46 to send the list to someone, or clear it 47.
  7. If the screen says that Network Monitor is off, press 49 to open Settings and turn it on.

The elements

Summary

1Active connectionTells you what you are connected to and whether it reaches the internet.

The headline reads like Wi-Fi - MyNetwork - Internet works. The last part says whether the internet works, whether the network wants you to sign in, whether you are connected without internet, or whether you are offline. With nothing connected the card says so and suggests turning on Wi-Fi or mobile data.

The readings are refreshed about once a second while this screen is open.

2Local IPThe address of your phone inside the network you are on.

Tap the row to copy the address. The row is shown only while something is connected. If the phone has no address yet, it says Unknown.

3External IPThe address the internet sees for you, shown only when you ask.

It stays hidden until you tap the row, because finding it means asking a public service on the internet. The row then says Asking.. and shows the address; tap it again to copy it. The address is kept only while the app runs and is never written to the history.

The same lookup is under Internet, where it comes with a notice first.

4Current speedHow much data is flowing to and from this phone right now.

Two figures: incoming with a down arrow and outgoing with an up arrow, counted for the whole phone, not just this app. It is a live reading, not a speed test; the graph is in the Internet section. The row is hidden while nothing is connected.

5Wi-FiOpens the Wi-Fi section.

The tile shows the network name and the link speed when the phone knows them. Its status line says Available, or why not: Permission not granted, Nothing connected right now, or No such hardware on this device. Before the first reading it says Open to check.

6MobileOpens the mobile network section.

The tile shows the name of the operator whose SIM is used for data. The status line works as on every tile.

7BluetoothOpens the Bluetooth section.

The tile shows how many devices are connected right now, for example Connected now: 2.

8SatellitesOpens the satellites section.

This tile uses the location receiver of the phone. If location is switched off in the system settings, the status line says so: Location is switched off in system settings.

9InternetOpens the Internet section with the path, the traffic and the checks.

Once you have looked up your external address in this session, the tile shows it.

10HistoryOpens the list of the measurements you ran.

Checks you start from Internet, Tools and Resource speed end up here.

11ToolsOpens ping, traceroute and the subnet scan.

These are for people who need to check a particular host or look at a local network.

12Resource SpeedOpens the speed test for one of your resources.

It measures how fast files move to and from a network or cloud resource you added to the app.

Wi-Fi, mobile network and Bluetooth

13Wi-Fi radioTurns the Wi-Fi radio on or off.

Android 10 and later usually do not let an app switch Wi-Fi by itself. When you tap the switch, the app tries, and if the system refuses it opens the system Wi-Fi panel instead; the switch moves when the panel changes the state. Where the device allows no switching at all, the switch is grayed out with a short explanation. If the state cannot be read it says State unknown.

Tap the section heading to open the system Wi-Fi settings.

14NetworkThe network you are connected to, its frequency, link speed and Wi-Fi standard.

The network name needs the precise location permission, because Android treats a Wi-Fi name as location data. Without it that row says Permission not granted. Tap to grant it, or Permission is blocked. Tap to open app settings, and the rest of the rows still work. When nothing is connected, the block is replaced by the reason.

15HotspotShows whether your phone is sharing its connection as a hotspot.

It reads Hotspot: On, Off or Not determined. Tap it to open the system hotspot settings. The row is hidden when the phone cannot tell.

16SignalA graph of the Wi-Fi signal strength over the last minutes.

One reading a second, in dBm; a value closer to 0 is a stronger signal. Under the graph you see now, lowest, highest and the trend: rising, falling or steady. Until the first readings arrive it says Collecting readings.. and the graph stays empty. It needs the precise location permission.

Tap the graph to restart it and begin collecting from now. A screen reader announces the restart.

17Link detailsThe technical details of the active connection.

Interface, IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, DNS servers, gateway, whether there is a default route, a proxy and the bandwidth estimates for incoming and outgoing data. A missing value reads Unknown. Handy when you need to tell a support person what your network looks like.

18SIM cardsHow many modems are active and which operator each SIM belongs to.

The number of active modems needs no permission. The SIM rows show the operator of SIM 1 and SIM 2 and mark the one preferred for data; they need the phone permission, and without it the section says Permission not granted. Tap to grant it. Each SIM gets its own signal graph that you can restart by tapping it.

19Open system network settingsOpens the system screen where mobile data and SIMs are controlled.

Android does not let an app switch mobile data or choose a SIM, and a note on the screen says so. This button takes you to the system network settings, where you can.

20Bluetooth radioTurns the Bluetooth radio on or off.

It works like the Wi-Fi switch: the app tries, and if the system refuses it opens the system Bluetooth settings. Tap the section heading to open them directly. The Adapter row says On, Off or Not on this device.

21Connected nowLists the devices connected now and the ones only paired.

Two lists: Connected now and Paired, not connected, each with a note when it is empty. Reading them needs the Bluetooth permission (Nearby devices on Android 12 and later); without it the lists say Permission not granted, and a tap asks for it.

22Chart a connected deviceChooses a connected device to draw its signal graph.

The list offers the devices connected now. Once you pick one, a Device signal graph appears and fills in once a second; tap the graph to restart it. With no connected device the field says there is nothing to chart.

Satellites and Internet

23Satellites in viewHow many satellites the phone sees, and how many it uses to fix its position.

Below the two counts is a line for each satellite: the system and number, the signal strength in dB-Hz, and whether it is counted in the position. A message says Searching for satellites.. until the first ones are found. A signal strength graph follows; tap it to restart.

This section needs the precise location permission and location switched on. Tapping the heading or either count opens the system location settings.

24PositionYour coordinates, how accurate they are and when they were fixed.

Latitude and longitude, the accuracy (for example ±5 m) and the time of the fix. Without a fix, or without the permission, the block gives the reason instead. The coordinates stay on the screen and are not sent anywhere.

25Record the track on this deviceRecords the path you move along while this screen is open.

The track stays on this device and is written only while the screen is open. Nothing is sent anywhere. While it records, a line counts the recorded points.

26Share the track fileSends the recorded track file to another app.

The button appears once there is a track file. It opens the system share sheet with a CSV file. If a newer recording has replaced the file, a note asks you to turn recording on and try again.

27Path to the internetDraws the way from your phone to the internet in four steps.

This device, Gateway (your router), DNS and Internet, each with its address or state and a mark showing whether it answers. Where the line breaks is where to look: the router, the name servers or the connection beyond them.

28Traffic on this deviceShows the traffic of the whole phone, now and over the last minutes.

Incoming now and Outgoing now, with a graph beneath. It counts everything the phone moves, not only this app. Tap the graph to restart it.

29What to checkChooses what the checks below look at: the internet or one of your resources.

The list starts with The internet and continues with the resources you added. Check the selected resource is available only when a resource is chosen.

30Check the selected resourceChecks whether the selected resource answers.

It tries to reach the resource with its saved address and credentials and says The resource answered, or that it did not, so you check the address and credentials. If the resource was deleted meanwhile, the result says so.

31Scan the local networkLooks for devices on your local network and the ports they have open.

Fill in the From and To addresses, or keep the range filled in for your own network. The result tells how many of the probed addresses answered and lists them with their open ports. A range that is too large or not a pair of addresses is refused with a hint. On newer Android versions the scan needs the local-network permission; if it is missing, the result tells you to grant it in the system settings and try again.

The scan contacts addresses on your network. Some office and campus networks treat that as unwanted, so the app shows a notice the first time in a session.

32Show the external addressAsks a public service for the address the internet sees.

The result shows the address and a note on whether it is your own or the operator translates it between its customers. If the router does not answer, the note says it can tell nothing either way. If no service answers, it says so and suggests trying again. The first time in a session a notice explains that the service sees the address it answers to.

33Measure the speedMeasures download and upload speed to the internet or to the chosen resource.

The test downloads and uploads about 20 MB in total, so a notice appears first. On a network the phone knows as metered the notice appears every time, and without your approval nothing is measured. The result shows both speeds in Mbps. If the test cannot finish, the result asks you to check the connection.

34StopStops the check that is running.

It appears with a progress bar while a check runs. Stop it at any time; the other checks stay unavailable until the running one finishes or is stopped.

Tools, resource speed and history

35IP or host nameThe address or name of the host to ping or trace.

Type an IP address or a host name. Below the field are chips with the hosts you used before: tap one to reuse it, tap its cross to forget it.

36PingSends a few small requests to the host and reports how fast it answers.

The console lists each attempt with its time in milliseconds, or No answer in time, and ends with how many of the attempts answered. A name that does not resolve or a missing network is reported in the console too.

37TracerouteShows the hops between your phone and the host.

Each hop that answers is listed with its time. The trace ends when the host is reached or after the maximum number of hops. Use it to see where a connection slows down or stops.

38Scan the local networkLooks for devices that answer in an address range.

It works like the scan in Internet, with the same From and To fields, and writes the answering addresses into the console. Use it only on networks that are yours.

39Console outputShows the results of ping, traceroute and the scan as text.

Before anything runs it says that results will appear here. While an operation runs, a progress bar with Stop appears above the console.

40CopyCopies the console text to the clipboard.

Use it to paste the results into a message or a note. With an empty console nothing is copied.

41ClearEmpties the console.

Only the text on the screen is removed. The measurements already written to the history stay there.

Undo
The console text is lost with no way back. Copy it first if you need it.
42Target ResourceChooses what to measure the speed to: the internet or one resource.

The list starts with The internet and continues with the network and cloud resources you added.

43Measure the speedMeasures how fast data moves to and from the chosen target.

It first checks that the target answers, then measures. A progress bar with Stop is shown while it runs. On a metered network a window asks first whether to go on.

44Speed ResultsThe download and upload speeds measured, in Mbps.

Download and Upload, each in megabits per second. A resource that is read-only shows (Read-only resource) in place of the upload, because uploading would write to it. Under the figures a status line says that the measurement finished, was stopped, or why it failed.

45HistoryThe measurements you ran, newest first.

Each row has the kind (Speed, Resource check, External address, Network scan, Ping or Traceroute), the network it ran on, the time and whether it answered. The list is kept on this device. While it is empty the screen says there are no measurements yet.

46ExportSends the whole list as a text file.

It opens the system share sheet with a plain text file. If the file cannot be prepared, a note asks you to free some space. The button is shown only when the list has rows.

47ClearDeletes every stored measurement.

A window asks first. The button is shown only when the list has rows.

Undo
Every measurement is removed from the device and cannot be restored. Export the list first if you still need it.

When Network Monitor is off

48Network Monitor is offTells you that Network Monitor is switched off in Settings.

Instead of the sections you see a short message and, where the feature exists, a button. In some editions Network Monitor is not part of the app at all; the message then says so and has no button. The screen does not show the message while it is still checking whether the monitor is on.

49Open settingsOpens Settings on the tab where Network Monitor is switched on.

It takes you to the Management tab, where the switch is under Additional programs and scenarios (the message on this screen calls it Operations). Turn it on, come back, and the summary appears.

Notice and confirmation windows

50ContinueGoes ahead with the check after you have read the notice.

The notice says what the check does: that the speed test uses about 20 MB of traffic, that the scan contacts your local network, that the external address lookup is passed to a public service, or that this network charges for traffic. The traffic, scan and address notices come once per session; the metered notice comes before every speed test.

51CancelCloses the notice and does not run the check.

Nothing is sent and nothing is measured.

52ClearConfirms that you want to clear the measurement history.

The window says the stored measurements will be removed from this device and suggests exporting first.

Undo
The measurements are deleted for good. Export them before you confirm if you may need them.
53CancelCloses the window and keeps the history.

Nothing is deleted.
