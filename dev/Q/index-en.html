Independent Review / multi-agent jury

How four AI agents judged the same audit

This page is a public summary of an independent comparison between four AI audits. It shows the final vote, the key differences in approach, and the short takeaways that help explain not just the winner, but the value of each participant.

Participants Claude, GPT, Codex, Gemini
Models Opus 4.7, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro
Material 4 independent verdicts
Format Comparative jury review
Conditions Shared rules and shared context
Final vote
3:1 for Claude

Three of the four judges ranked Claude first. One vote went to GPT.

Practical takeaway

Claude emerged as the strongest full-picture result, while GPT is especially valuable where strong verification, measurement, and tighter factual grounding matter most.

Context

What this experiment was

Four AI agents received the same audit task. After finishing their own work, each agent separately reviewed the final materials and selected the strongest result. The comparison was not abstract: the models mattered, but so did the fact that all participants worked under closely matched conditions.

Technical lineup

The comparison included Claude Code on Opus 4.7 with a 1M context window and high effort, Microsoft Copilot on GPT-5.4 in XHigh mode, Codex on GPT-5.5 in XHigh mode, and Gemini 3.1 Pro in High mode. All four participants were used in their strongest available configurations. Two systems used GPT-family models, but through different providers and different agent shells.

Equal conditions

All participants received the same prompt chain. The comparison also started from the same working base: similar agents, the same skills and rules, and an already accumulated history of working with this project.

First stage

Each agent had to study the working environment, the project, and the process, then propose practical recommendations for speeding up work and reducing wasted effort.

Second stage

Once the audits were complete, a separate comparison round began: every participant had to pick the strongest result and explain why.

Why this page matters

It lets you see the overall outcome quickly, without reading four long evaluations in sequence, and helps surface where opinions converged and where they diverged.

Consensus

What kept repeating across judges

These patterns surfaced in multiple verdicts, which makes them the most stable and meaningful parts of the comparison.

Claude won on depth and cohesion

  • It revised earlier hypotheses instead of simply repeating them more confidently.
  • It focused not only on problems, but also on the cost of delaying decisions.
  • It connected technical observations to the way the working process actually behaves.

GPT stood out on facts and checks

  • It offered the strongest factual base and held more tightly to verifiable details.
  • It was good at spotting cases where default rules should be challenged.
  • It used process history well, not just the current project snapshot.

Codex was stable and practical

  • It structured tools, steps, and engineering discipline clearly.
  • It was strong as an action plan, though less likely to produce surprising strategic insights.

Gemini lost on confidence and depth

  • The verdict was shorter and less fully argued than the others.
  • It contained fewer concrete, verifiable findings.
Detailed Verdicts

Expanded judge positions

The tabs below show not only the winners, but the reasoning behind each choice, the strengths of the finalists, and the limits of each approach.

Claude's choice: Claude

How the Claude judge thinks

This judge values not only the quality of recommendations, but also an author's ability to revise their own conclusions, see the structure of the problem, and keep attention on what matters most.

  • Self-correction ranks above smooth but linear confidence.
  • The strongest unique point was the gap between idea generation and actual implementation.
  • A clear statement of what not to do was treated as a major strength.

What Claude valued most in GPT

Dense factual support, measurable reasoning, and the ability to strengthen conclusions with concrete checks rather than general commentary.

The weak side of Claude's logic

This approach is more willing to tolerate a longer text if it produces a richer and more layered conclusion.

GPT's choice: GPT

How the GPT judge thinks

The top priority here is a document that works as a complete, self-sufficient report: dense, clear, verifiable, and useful without extra assembly.

  • The main criterion is how strongly each claim rests on observable evidence.
  • The winner is the one that best combines problem history, measurable signals, and practical conclusions.
  • Claude ranks second because it is stronger conceptually, but less compact as a single working document.

What GPT valued most in Claude

Depth of thinking, a sharper view of process design, and the ability to notice the hidden cost of repeated decisions.

The weak side of GPT's logic

This approach rewards proof and compactness more strongly, which can leave rarer strategic insights slightly undervalued.

Codex's choice: Claude

How the Codex judge thinks

This judge looks at reasoning in an applied way: who saw not just isolated issues, but the shape of the process, the cost of wrong hypotheses, and the importance of doing things in the right order.

  • Claude wins on cohesion, self-reflection, and strong prioritization.
  • Its own result is seen as useful and engineering-focused, but less surprising in its conclusions.
  • GPT ranks lower not because it is weak, but because its strengths feel more narrowly focused.

What Codex sees as its own strength

A strong working plan, engineering discipline, and close attention to how recommendations translate into repeatable steps.

The weak side of Codex's logic

Compared with GPT, it gives less weight to quantitative proof and more to reasoning quality and decision structure.

Gemini's choice: Claude

How the Gemini judge thinks

The reasoning here is simpler than the others, but it points in the same direction: the top score goes to the author who understood the process best and offered the most useful conclusions for speeding up work.

  • It highlighted iteration speed, accumulated noise, and the impact of frequent interface shifts on efficiency.
  • GPT placed second for strong use of facts and process behavior patterns.
  • Gemini's own result is effectively acknowledged as the weakest in depth and persuasiveness.

What Gemini confirms as useful

This is another sign that Claude's lead was not accidental: even a shorter review still lands on the same overall conclusion.

The weak side of Gemini's logic

The argument is shorter and the comparison less deep, so this verdict is most useful as additional confirmation of the wider pattern.

Takeaways

What is actually worth carrying forward

The main value of this page is not the debate itself, but the short map of conclusions that can be used from here.

1. Foundation

If you want the strongest overall result, Claude is the winner because it best connects technical observations with the structure of the working process.

2. Factual layer

If you need facts, measurements, and strict claim validation, GPT is especially useful.

3. Execution layer

Codex is especially useful where step ordering, engineering discipline, and careful practical follow-through matter most.

4. Anti-noise lesson

The core lesson is simple: endless discussion is worse than a short list of concrete implementations carried through to completion.

The next sensible step is to turn the shared conclusions into a short action list with priorities, cost, and implementation status.
