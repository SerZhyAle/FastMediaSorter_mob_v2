Every external surface of the product is written **from this file**: the landing pages, the README and its translations, the public feature showcase, the store listings, the screenshot captions. No surface is ever written from another surface - if two pages disagree, this file decides which one is wrong.

The pillar order below is fixed. Wherever a surface lists what the product does, it lists these eight in this order.

---

## 1. What the product is

**FastMediaSorter is a complete shell for an Android device: it takes over the home screen, plays your media, opens live streams, launches your apps, talks to your watch, keeps an eye on the device, and manages every file you own.**

You install one app and stop needing several. It sits where the stock launcher used to sit, so everything starts from it: the photos and videos on the phone, the films on the network drive, the radio and TV streams from the internet, the apps you use every day, the watch on your wrist. Nothing has to be handed to another program to be opened. Managing and sorting files across local, network and cloud storage is part of what it does - it is the foundation the rest is built on, not the whole of it.

---

## 2. The eight pillars

1. **Device shell.** It replaces the launcher and becomes the home screen, so the device starts in your layout instead of the manufacturer's.
2. **Media player.** Photos, video, music and books open and play inside the app, with no hand-off to another program.
3. **Live streams.** Internet radio, TV and camera feeds sit beside your own files as an equal source of content.
4. **App launching.** Your installed apps are listed, grouped and started from the shell, with the ones you use most within reach.
5. **A replacement for stock apps.** Gallery, music player, file manager, launcher, reader - after installing this, several of them stop being needed.
6. **A companion on the watch.** The Wear OS side is a full half of the product: your content and controls follow you onto the wrist, not a notification mirror.
7. **Device monitoring.** Storage, battery, network and what is running are visible without leaving the shell.
8. **A full file manager.** Copy, move, rename, delete, and of course sort - across the phone, network shares and cloud accounts, with the same tools everywhere.

## 3. How to use this file

- **Do not copy sentences verbatim** into a store listing or a landing page. Each surface has its own genre and its own length limit. Carry the **claims** across, not the wording.
- **Keep the order.** A surface that lists the pillars lists them 1 to 8. A surface too short for all eight names the first two or three.
- **File management is eighth**, except in the Google Play full description, where it has to be named as a core function in the opening block - the All Files Access permission is granted against that statement, and a listing that read as a media player was rejected once already. See `store_assets/PLAY_PERMISSIONS_DECLARATION.md`.
- **Write for a person, not a developer.** "Device shell" is explained by what someone does with it, never by an architecture term. Tone rules: `COMMUNICATION_POLICY.md`.
- **Change this file first.** A surface whose wording turns out to be wrong is fixed here before it is fixed there, or the drift starts again.
